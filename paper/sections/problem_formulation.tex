\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem_formulation}

\subsection{System Model}

The system consists of a Boston Dynamics Spot quadruped and a Unitree Z1
serial manipulator arm rigidly mounted on the robot's back. Four reference
frames are defined: the world frame $\mathcal{F}_W$, the Spot body frame
$\mathcal{F}_B$ (origin at the geometric centre of the base), the arm base
frame $\mathcal{F}_0$ (coincident with Z1 link~0, fixed relative to
$\mathcal{F}_B$), and the end-effector frame $\mathcal{F}_{EE}$ (at the
Z1 flange, where the ultrasound probe is attached).

\subsubsection{Spot base kinematics}
The Spot base is modelled as a non-holonomic vehicle constrained to move on a
planar surface. Its configuration is described by
$\boldsymbol{\xi} = [x,\, y,\, \theta]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^2 \times
\mathbb{S}^1$, where $(x, y)$ is the position of $\mathcal{F}_B$ in
$\mathcal{F}_W$ and $\theta$ is the yaw angle. The non-holonomic constraint
(no lateral slip) restricts the admissible base velocities to
\begin{equation}
  \dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}} =
  \begin{bmatrix} \dot{x} \\ \dot{y} \\ \dot{\theta} \end{bmatrix}
  =
  \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta \\ \sin\theta \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} v_x
  +
  \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \omega_z,
  \label{eq:nonholonomic}
\end{equation}
where $v_x \in \mathbb{R}$ is the forward linear velocity and
$\omega_z \in \mathbb{R}$ is the yaw angular velocity, both expressed in
$\mathcal{F}_B$.

\subsubsection{Z1 arm kinematics}
The Z1 arm has $n = 6$ revolute joints with configuration vector
$\mathbf{q} = [q_1, \ldots, q_6]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^6$ and joint velocity
vector $\dot{\mathbf{q}} \in \mathbb{R}^6$. The forward kinematics map
$\mathbf{T}_{EE}(\mathbf{q}) \in SE(3)$ gives the homogeneous transformation
from $\mathcal{F}_0$ to $\mathcal{F}_{EE}$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{T}_{EE}(\mathbf{q}) =
  \prod_{i=1}^{6} \mathbf{T}_{i-1,i}(q_i),
  \label{eq:fk}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{T}_{i-1,i}(q_i) \in SE(3)$ is the transformation between
consecutive link frames. The arm geometric Jacobian $\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{arm}}
(\mathbf{q}) \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 6}$ relates joint velocities to the
spatial velocity of $\mathcal{F}_{EE}$ expressed in $\mathcal{F}_0$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}_{EE}^{\mathrm{arm}} =
  \mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{arm}}(\mathbf{q})\,\dot{\mathbf{q}},
  \label{eq:arm_jacobian}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{v}_{EE}^{\mathrm{arm}} =
[\boldsymbol{\omega}^\top,\, \dot{\mathbf{p}}^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^6$
is the concatenation of angular velocity $\boldsymbol{\omega} \in \mathbb{R}^3$
and linear velocity $\dot{\mathbf{p}} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ of the \ac{EE}.

\subsubsection{Unified decision variable}
The combined control input is defined as
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{u} =
  \begin{bmatrix} \dot{\mathbf{q}} \\ v_x \\ \omega_z \end{bmatrix}
  \in \mathbb{R}^8,
  \label{eq:decision_var}
\end{equation}
subject to the velocity bounds
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{u}_{\min} \leq \mathbf{u} \leq \mathbf{u}_{\max},
  \label{eq:bounds}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{u}_{\min}, \mathbf{u}_{\max} \in \mathbb{R}^8$ encode joint
velocity limits for the arm and maximum linear and angular speed for the base.

\subsection{Task Definition}

An autonomous \ac{FAST} scan requires the \ac{EE} to reach a sequence of
$N$ anatomical target poses. Let
$\mathcal{T} = \{\mathbf{T}_i^* \in SE(3)\}_{i=1}^{N}$, with $N = 4$,
denote the desired \ac{EE} poses corresponding to the four \ac{FAST} windows:
subxiphoid, right upper quadrant, left upper quadrant, and suprapubic.
Each target pose $\mathbf{T}_i^* = (\mathbf{R}_i^*, \mathbf{p}_i^*)$
comprises a desired \ac{EE} position $\mathbf{p}_i^* \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and
orientation $\mathbf{R}_i^* \in SO(3)$, both expressed in $\mathcal{F}_W$.

The target positions are not known a priori. During the approach phase, a
patient position estimate $\hat{\mathbf{p}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^3$, obtained
from the \ac{YOLO}+\ac{KF} perception pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:system_architecture}),
is used to compute a dynamically updated intermediate goal for the \ac{WBC}.
Once the robot reaches the scanning distance, the \ac{FAST} target poses
$\mathcal{T}$ are determined from the surface geometry estimated by the
\ac{EE}-mounted RealSense camera.

\subsection{Problem Statement}

Given the system model defined in~\eqref{eq:nonholonomic}--\eqref{eq:decision_var}
and the target set $\mathcal{T}$, the autonomous \ac{FAST} scanning problem
is to find a control sequence $\mathbf{u}(t)$ that:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Navigation}: drives the Spot base from its initial pose
        $\boldsymbol{\xi}_0$ to a configuration from which all targets in
        $\mathcal{T}$ lie within the reachable workspace of the Z1 arm,
        using the online patient estimate $\hat{\mathbf{p}}(t)$;

  \item \textbf{Probe placement}: brings the \ac{EE} to each target pose
        $\mathbf{T}_i^* \in \mathcal{T}$ in sequence, satisfying
        \begin{equation}
          \|\mathbf{p}_{EE}(t_i) - \mathbf{p}_i^*\| \leq \varepsilon_p,
          \quad
          d\!\left(\mathbf{R}_{EE}(t_i),\, \mathbf{R}_i^*\right) \leq \varepsilon_r,
          \label{eq:task_constraints}
        \end{equation}
        where $\mathbf{p}_{EE}$ and $\mathbf{R}_{EE}$ are the current \ac{EE}
        position and orientation, $\varepsilon_p > 0$ is the admissible
        position error, $\varepsilon_r > 0$ is the admissible orientation
        error, and $d(\cdot,\cdot)$ denotes a rotation distance on $SO(3)$;

  \item \textbf{Contact}: maintains the probe in compliant contact with the
        patient's skin during scanning, with normal contact force
        $f_c \in [f_{\min},\, f_{\max}]$, where $f_{\min} > 0$ ensures
        acoustic coupling and $f_{\max}$ is the patient-safety limit;

  \item \textbf{Input feasibility}: satisfies the velocity bounds
        in~\eqref{eq:bounds} at all times.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
The following assumptions are adopted throughout this paper:
\begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{A1}] The patient remains stationary during the scanning phase.
  \item[\textbf{A2}] Spot operates on flat terrain, so the non-holonomic
        model~\eqref{eq:nonholonomic} is valid.
  \item[\textbf{A3}] The rigid transform between $\mathcal{F}_B$ and
        $\mathcal{F}_0$ is known and constant (fixed mount).
  \item[\textbf{A4}] A single patient is present in the scene.
\end{itemize}
