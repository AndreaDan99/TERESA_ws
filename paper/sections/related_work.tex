\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

This section reviews the three main research threads that \ac{TERESA} builds
upon: autonomous robotic ultrasound, whole-body control for legged
manipulators, and active perception for mobile manipulation. We conclude with
a review of posture optimisation and body reconfiguration techniques for
mobile manipulation.

\subsection{Robotic Ultrasound Systems}

Robot-assisted ultrasound has been an active research area for over two
decades, driven by the need to reduce operator variability, enable remote
diagnostics, and automate repetitive scanning procedures. Comprehensive
surveys by von Haxthausen~\cite{vonhaxthausen2021}, Li et
al.~\cite{li2021overview}, and Munir et al.~\cite{munir2025} document the
evolution from early teleoperated systems to contemporary autonomous scanning
platforms. Hidalgo et al.~\cite{hidalgo2023} review current clinical
applications of robot-assisted ultrasound, highlighting both the progress and
the persistent limitation that all deployed systems operate on fixed bases.

Two technical challenges have dominated the literature: probe positioning
accuracy and contact force control. Gilbertson and
Anthony~\cite{gilbertson2015} established a force/position decoupling
framework for freehand ultrasound. Dyck et al.~\cite{dyck2022} extended
impedance control to arbitrary curved surfaces using discrete differential
geometry. Ning et al.~\cite{ning2021} proposed force-guided scanning for soft
tissue environments. Ma et al.~\cite{ma2022} introduced an active-sensing
end-effector that enables the probe to self-normalise to the skin
surface---an early form of perception-driven adaptation.

On the planning side, Suligoj et al.~\cite{suligoj2021} presented RobUSt, a
system that autonomously acquires medical images following a pre-defined
anatomical protocol. Duan et al.~\cite{duan2022} demonstrated
optimisation-based control for scoliosis assessment with variable impedance.
In our own prior work~\cite{farsoni2025autonomous}, we showed that a
collaborative robot arm on a fixed base can autonomously screen the abdominal
aorta using impedance control and deep-learning-based segmentation.

A common thread across all these systems is the assumption that the patient
is pre-positioned within the arm's reachable workspace. No existing
autonomous robotic ultrasound system has been deployed on a legged mobile
platform capable of navigating to an unknown patient location in an
unstructured environment. \ac{TERESA} addresses this gap by mounting the
ultrasound payload on a Boston Dynamics Spot quadruped and providing the
full perception-to-scanning pipeline needed for autonomous operation.

\subsection{Whole-Body Control for Legged Manipulators}

\ac{WBC} has emerged as the dominant paradigm for coordinating locomotion and
manipulation on legged robots. Khatib~\cite{khatib1987} introduced the
operational space formulation, and Yoshikawa~\cite{yoshikawa1985} defined the
manipulability measure that underpins singularity avoidance in most modern
\ac{WBC} approaches. Bayle et al.~\cite{bayle2003} extended manipulability
analysis to wheeled mobile manipulators, showing that base motion can
preserve arm dexterity.

A comprehensive survey by Wensing et al.~\cite{wensing2023} covers
optimisation-based control for dynamic legged robots, including \ac{WBC} and
\ac{MPC} formulations. For legged platforms with mounted arms, several
hierarchical and holistic approaches have been proposed. Bellicoso et
al.~\cite{bellicoso2019} demonstrated articulated locomotion and manipulation
(ALMA) on a torque-controlled quadruped. Minniti et al.~\cite{minniti2019}
formulated whole-body \ac{MPC} for dynamically stable mobile manipulation,
and Sleiman et al.~\cite{sleiman2021} unified locomotion and manipulation
into a single \ac{MPC}. Haviland et al.~\cite{haviland2022} proposed a
holistic reactive \ac{WBC} that simultaneously optimises arm and base
velocities through a single \ac{QP}. Li et al.~\cite{li2022wbc} presented a
hierarchical \ac{QP}-based \ac{WBC} for a torque-controlled quadrupedal
manipulator, and Liu et al.~\cite{liu2024visual} incorporated visual feedback
into the \ac{WBC} loop for loco-manipulation tasks. On the Boston Dynamics
Spot platform, Zimmermann et al.~\cite{zimmermann2021} demonstrated dynamic
grasping with an external arm, and Xie et al.~\cite{xie2024manip} proposed
adaptive motion distribution to enhance manipulability on legged
manipulators.

Wolfslag et al.~\cite{wolfslag2020} studied optimisation of body-ground
contact to augment whole-body loco-manipulation on quadruped robots,
introducing the concept that body posture directly affects manipulation
capability. This insight is central to \ac{TERESA}'s body reconfiguration
strategy.

Despite these advances, all existing legged \ac{WBC} formulations share a
critical assumption: the manipulation target is known in the world frame a
priori. They do not address the perceptual uncertainty inherent to detecting
and approaching an unknown patient in an unstructured emergency scenario.
\ac{TERESA} extends \ac{WBC} by closing the loop between perception quality
and body posture: the robot actively reconfigures itself to improve
detection, using \ac{WBC} for reactive arm look-at during approach and
pre-planned body reconfiguration for scanning.

\subsection{Active Perception for Mobile Manipulation}

Active perception---the principle that a robot should move to improve its
sensory input---has been studied extensively in computer vision and robotics.
Zeng et al.~\cite{zeng2020} provide a comprehensive survey of view planning
in robot active vision, covering systems, algorithms, and applications. Bai
et al.~\cite{bai2021} survey information-driven path planning, where motion
is optimised to maximise information gain from onboard sensors.

In the context of mobile manipulation, Zaky et al.~\cite{zaky2020}
demonstrated that active perception strategies consistently outperform
passive observation for goal-oriented manipulation tasks. Zhang and
Scaramuzza~\cite{zhang2020fisher} introduced the Fisher Information Field, a
differentiable map that quantifies perceptual quality and can be integrated
into motion planning. Tordesillas and How~\cite{tordesillas2022} proposed
PANTHER, a perception-aware trajectory planner that optimises the vehicle's
yaw to maintain visibility of dynamic obstacles, demonstrating the value of
coupling perception constraints with motion optimisation.

Next-best-view planning has been applied to manipulation tasks. Breyer et
al.~\cite{breyer2022} presented a closed-loop next-best-view planner for
target-driven grasping, in which the robot continuously selects viewpoints
that maximise information gain about the target object. Hu and
Pagilla~\cite{hu2022} proposed view planning for active robot perception
using point clouds, optimising sensor poses for object pose estimation. Ren
and Qureshi~\cite{ren2023} developed active neural sensing and planning for
robot manipulators operating in unknown cluttered environments, combining
deep-learning-based scene understanding with viewpoint optimisation.

The closest works to our approach combine active perception with whole-body
motion. Mittal et al.~\cite{mittal2022} demonstrated whole-body mobile
manipulation for articulated object interaction in unknown scenes, including
experiments on legged platforms. Jauhri et al.~\cite{jauhri2024} proposed
active-perceptive motion generation for mobile manipulation, where a
receding-horizon planner generates informative trajectories that balance
task progress with perceptual quality. Xie et al.~\cite{xie2024capm}
introduced coupled active perception and manipulation planning for a mobile
manipulator in agricultural settings, explicitly constraining the robot's
state by perceptual requirements. Qin et al.~\cite{qin2026} recently proposed
a one-shot multimodal active perception framework that learns to predict
optimal viewpoints for robotic manipulation.

However, none of these systems address the specific challenge of whole-body
reconfiguration on a legged platform for medical tasks. Existing active
perception frameworks operate on wheeled bases (Mittal et al., Jauhri et al.)
or fixed manipulators (Ren and Qureshi), where the perception-action loop is
limited to camera viewpoint or arm pose. On a legged platform, body height,
pitch, and yaw can all be controlled to reshape the perceptual field---a
richer action space that \ac{TERESA} exploits through confidence-gated search
and pre-planned body reconfiguration.

\subsection{Posture Optimisation and Body Reconfiguration}

Optimising the base pose of a mobile manipulator to maximise task performance
has been studied in the context of reachability and manipulability. Makhal and
Goins~\cite{makhal2018} introduced Reuleaux, a method for robot base placement
via reachability analysis, generating maps of feasible end-effector positions
as a function of base location. Fan et al.~\cite{fan2021} proposed base
position optimisation for machining large components, demonstrating that
pre-planned base placement significantly improves manipulation quality. Zhao
et al.~\cite{zhao2025} presented B*, an efficient optimal base placement
algorithm for fixed-base manipulators.

Gong et al.~\cite{gong2023} reviewed legged robots for object manipulation,
identifying body posture optimisation as a key open challenge. Wolfslag et
al.~\cite{wolfslag2020} explicitly optimised body-ground contact configurations
on a quadruped to enhance whole-body loco-manipulation, showing that even small
adjustments in body height and orientation can substantially affect arm
reachability.

\ac{TERESA} extends these ideas by embedding posture optimisation within an
active perception loop. Rather than optimising base placement for a known
task, the system first uses body posture to improve perceptual quality
(Section~\ref{sec:active_perception}), and then pre-plans body
reconfiguration to bring each anatomical scanning target into the arm's
optimal workspace (Section~\ref{sec:method}). This two-stage approach allows
the legged platform to serve both as a perception aid and as a manipulation
enabler, closing the gap between active perception and whole-body
reconfiguration for autonomous medical assessment.
