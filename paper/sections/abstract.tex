\begin{abstract}

Emergency assessment protocols such as \ac{FAST} ultrasound and the \ac{ABCDE}
survey are critical for rapid triage in mass-casualty incidents, disaster
sites, and scenarios where trained clinicians are unavailable or overwhelmed.
Deploying a legged mobile manipulator to autonomously perform these assessments
could extend emergency care to inaccessible environments, but poses a
fundamental challenge: the robot must actively reconfigure its body to
compensate for perceptual uncertainty while navigating toward and scanning an
unknown patient.

This paper presents \ac{TERESA} (Trustworthy Emergency Robot for Efficient
Support and Assistance), an integrated system for autonomous emergency
assessment mounted on a Boston Dynamics Spot legged robot equipped with a
Unitree Z1 manipulator arm. The core of \ac{TERESA} is a whole-body active
perception framework in which the robot continuously adapts its posture---
body height, pitch, and yaw---to maximise perceptual quality and bring
anatomical targets into the arm's optimal workspace. A confidence-gated
active search phase explores a $3{\times}3$ body-pose grid, freezing only
when detection confidence exceeds \SI{0.85}{}; an anticipatory multi-view
body scanner acquires patient anatomy during navigation, eliminating the idle
time of traditional stop-then-scan approaches; and a perception-quality
monitor scales approach velocity in proportion to detection confidence,
ensuring safe and efficient navigation. During scanning, a pre-planned body
reconfiguration optimises Spot's posture for each anatomical target through
an offline grid search over body height and pitch, maximising arm
manipulability without requiring real-time whole-body optimisation.

Experiments on the physical Spot+Z1 platform demonstrate that \ac{TERESA}
achieves fully autonomous emergency assessment, with the active perception
pipeline reducing mission idle time by \XX\,\% compared to a sequential
stop-and-manipulate baseline while maintaining a probe positioning error
below \XX\,mm. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first legged robotic
system to demonstrate perception-driven whole-body reconfiguration for
autonomous emergency assessment.

\end{abstract}
