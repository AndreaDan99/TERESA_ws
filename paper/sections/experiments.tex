\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Hardware Platform}

\ac{TERESA} is built on a Boston Dynamics Spot quadruped equipped with a
Unitree Z1 collaborative arm mounted on the rear payload deck. The primary
perception sensor is an Orbbec Femto Bolt \ac{RGBD} camera with an
\SI{87}{\degree} horizontal field of view, rigidly attached to the front of
the Spot chassis at a height of approximately \SI{0.5}{\metre} above the
ground. A secondary Intel RealSense D435 depth camera is mounted on the Z1
end-effector, providing close-range torso tracking during the approach and
scanning phases. All computation runs on an onboard Intel NUC 13 Pro
(i7-1360P) with an NVIDIA RTX 4060 Ti GPU for real-time inference. The
software stack is implemented in ROS 2 Humble, with the whole-body controller
running at \SI{100}{\hertz} via a \ac{QP} solver configured from the
parameters in \texttt{wbc\_params.yaml}.

\subsection{Search Time Experiment}

The first experiment quantifies the advantage of the Z1 arm during the
initial patient-search phase. We conducted 50 physical trials on the Spot+Z1
platform with a human subject lying on the ground at predefined orientations.
The subject's body yaw angle $\theta$ (defined as the angle between the
subject's head-to-foot axis and the robot's initial heading) was varied across
five canonical values: $0\degree$, $45\degree$, $90\degree$, $135\degree$, and
$180\degree$.  For each angle, five trials were performed in each of two
configurations:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Spot+Arm (TERESA)}: the full search pipeline described in
        Section~\ref{sec:confidence_search}---three base yaw positions
        ($0\degree$, $+30\degree$, $-30\degree$) with the arm executing
        seven search poses per position.
  \item \textbf{Orbbec-Only}: the Z1 arm is disabled; only the
        front-mounted Orbbec Femto Bolt camera is active, and the base
        must physically rotate through five discrete yaw positions
        (spaced by the camera's effective FOV of \SI{77}{\degree}) for
        full \SI{360}{\degree} coverage.
\end{itemize}

Trial order was randomised to mitigate learning effects.  Before each trial,
the Spot base was reset to its initial yaw (\SI{0}{\degree}) and the
odometry frame was re-initialised.  The search time was measured from the
moment the robot entered the SEARCHING state until either (a) the patient
was locked (Orbbec LYING confidence $\geq$ \num{0.70} with a valid approach
point) or (b) the search cycle completed without detection (recorded as a
failure).  All other system parameters---base yaw velocity
(\SI{0.2}{\radian\per\second}, \texttt{search\_max\_angular\_vel}), arm pose
timeout (\SI{1.2}{\second}, \texttt{search\_timeout\_per\_point}), and
search yaw angles ($\pm\SI{30}{\degree}$, \texttt{search\_yaw\_angles})---
matched the production configuration in \texttt{wbc\_params.yaml}.
Results are reported as mean $\pm$ standard deviation over the five
repetitions at each angle.
