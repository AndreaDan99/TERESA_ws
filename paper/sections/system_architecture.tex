\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:system_architecture}

Fig.~\ref{fig:system_block} gives an overview of the full \ac{TERESA}
system. The architecture is organised into four layers: sensors,
perception, mission management, and control. All components communicate
over \ac{ROS}~2 with \ac{DDS}, distributed across two compute units:
a NVIDIA Jetson (perception and arm control) and the Spot onboard
computer SpotCore (base control and locomotion).

\subsection{Hardware Platform}

The \ac{TERESA} platform consists of three main hardware components.
The \textbf{Boston Dynamics Spot} quadruped provides mobile navigation
on unstructured terrain. It is commanded via its onboard \ac{ROS}~2
driver (\texttt{spot\_ros2}) through high-level velocity commands
$[v_x, \omega_z]$ on the \texttt{/cmd\_vel} topic. The \textbf{Unitree
Z1} is a six-\ac{DOF} serial manipulator with a payload of
\SI{2}{\kilogram}, mounted rigidly on Spot's back. It is controlled at
the torque level through a \ac{JTC} interface running on the Jetson.
The \textbf{ultrasound probe} is attached to the Z1 flange
(\ac{EE} frame $\mathcal{F}_{EE}$) and serves as the sensing element
for the \ac{FAST} scan.

\subsection{Sensor Suite}

Two depth cameras cover complementary fields of view:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Orbbec RGB-D}: mounted on the Spot body, provides a
        wide field of view for patient detection at distances up to
        \SI{3}{\metre}. It feeds the \ac{YOLO}+\ac{KF} perception
        pipeline during the approach phase.

  \item \textbf{Intel RealSense D435}: mounted at the Z1 \ac{EE},
        provides close-range depth and colour for surface normal
        estimation. It is active during the scanning phase once the
        probe is within \SI{30}{\centi\metre} of the patient.
\end{itemize}

The static transform between the Spot body frame $\mathcal{F}_B$ and
the arm base frame $\mathcal{F}_0$ is a calibrated rigid offset
published as a static \ac{TF} at system startup.

\subsection{Perception Pipeline}

\subsubsection{Wide-field patient localisation (Orbbec)}
During the approach phase, \ac{YOLO}11-pose~\cite{maji2022} runs on
the Orbbec \ac{RGBD} stream at \SI{30}{\hertz} and detects the
skeleton of the patient. The torso position is computed as the
centroid of four \ac{COCO} keypoints---left shoulder, right shoulder,
left hip, right hip---by back-projecting their image coordinates
through the calibrated depth map. The resulting 3D measurement feeds
the \texttt{Kalman3D} filter (state: position + velocity,
$\mathbf{x}_{KF} \in \mathbb{R}^6$), which predicts the torso
position at every frame and updates only when a valid detection is
available. The filtered estimate $\hat{\mathbf{p}}(t)$ is published
to the \ac{WBC} as the approach target.

\subsubsection{Close-range surface estimation (RealSense)}
Once the \ac{FSM} transitions to the HANDOFF state, the RealSense
becomes the primary sensor. A surface estimation node fits a plane to
the depth point cloud in a region of interest around the detected
torso, and publishes the surface centroid $\mathbf{p}_s$ and outward
normal $\mathbf{n}_s$ as a \texttt{PoseStamped} message. These are
used by the impedance controller to compute the desired probe position
via~\eqref{eq:surface_target}.

\subsection{Mission FSM}

The mission is orchestrated by a six-state \ac{FSM} implemented in the
\texttt{wbc\_coordinator} node. Fig.~\ref{fig:fsm} shows the state
diagram.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{\input{figures/fsm}}
  \caption{Mission \ac{FSM}. Blue states have the \ac{WBC} \ac{QP}
           active; the green state uses the impedance controller.
           APPR.~=~APPROACHING, CONF.~=~CONFIRMING,
           WS\_EXT~=~WS\_EXTENSION.}
  \label{fig:fsm}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{IDLE}: system inactive, waiting for a valid patient
        detection from the Orbbec pipeline.

  \item \textbf{APPROACHING}: \ac{WBC} \ac{QP} active. Spot navigates
        toward the patient while the Z1 arm pre-positions the probe
        in the direction of $\hat{\mathbf{p}}(t)$. The \ac{QP} target
        is updated every \SI{1.5}{\second} to respect the minimum
        trajectory time of the arm \ac{JTC}.

  \item \textbf{CONFIRMING}: Orbbec detection confidence drops below
        threshold. The arm holds its last estimate; the \ac{WBC}
        remains active but the goal is frozen. If confidence recovers,
        the \ac{FSM} returns to APPROACHING; if the patient is
        completely lost, it transitions to HANDOFF.

  \item \textbf{HANDOFF}: Orbbec loses the patient entirely. Spot
        halts. Control is transferred to the RealSense pipeline, which
        must lock onto the torso surface before the \ac{FSM} can
        advance.

  \item \textbf{SCANNING}: \ac{WBC} \ac{QP} dormant. The impedance
        controller drives the probe through the \ac{FAST} scan
        sequence using the surface frame from the RealSense. If a
        target is reported unreachable, the \ac{FSM} triggers a
        workspace extension.

  \item \textbf{WS\_EXTENSION}: the \ac{WBC} \ac{QP} reactivates to
        execute a micro-step that repositions Spot so that the target
        re-enters the Z1 workspace. Once the arm \ac{IK} confirms
        success (\texttt{/ik\_done}), the \ac{FSM} returns to
        SCANNING.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{IK Goal Multiplexer}

The Z1 arm \ac{IK}-to-\ac{JTC} node receives a single
\texttt{/ik\_goal\_pose} topic. Since both the \ac{WBC} \ac{QP} and
the z1\_FSM scan controller publish arm targets, an \texttt{ik\_goal\_mux}
node arbitrates between them based on a binary enable signal
(\texttt{/wbc/enable}) from the \ac{FSM}: when \ac{WBC} is active its
target takes priority; otherwise the z1\_FSM target is forwarded.
This design preserves the existing arm control stack unchanged.
