\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:system_architecture}

The \ac{TERESA} system is organised into four layers---hardware, perception,
mission management, and control---communicating over \ac{ROS}~2 with
\ac{DDS}. Computation is distributed across three units: the Spot onboard
computer SpotCore (base control and odometry), a NVIDIA Jetson (perception and
arm control), and an external PC (mission coordination and \ac{WBC}).

\subsection{Hardware Platform}

The Boston Dynamics Spot quadruped is commanded via its onboard \ac{ROS}~2
driver through velocity commands $[v_x,\, \omega_z]$ and body posture commands.
The Unitree Z1 is a six-\ac{DOF} manipulator mounted on Spot's back, controlled
at the torque level through a \ac{JTC} interface. Two \ac{RGBD} cameras provide
complementary coverage. An Orbbec Femto Bolt is mounted on Spot's body for
wide-field patient detection at distances up to \SI{3}{\metre}. An Intel
RealSense D435 is mounted at the Z1 \ac{EE}; the arm's \ac{WBC} look-at
controller positions it as an active pan-tilt unit for close-range torso
tracking and surface estimation. Spot's five built-in stereo cameras are
positioned low on the body for ground-plane obstacle avoidance and are not
suitable for \ac{YOLO} skeleton detection.

\subsection{Mission Finite State Machine}

The mission is orchestrated by a nine-state \ac{FSM} shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:fsm}. The robot waits for \ac{TF} readiness, then upon the start
command enters SEARCHING, sweeping through body yaw orientations while two
cameras observe the environment. A RealSense detection triggers SEMI\_LOCKING,
rotating the base toward the torso to give the Orbbec a clean observation
window. An Orbbec confirmation transitions to LOCKING, where approach point
samples are collected. PRE\_APPROACH straightens Spot while the \ac{WBC}
look-at controller orients the RealSense toward the patient's torso.
APPROACHING drives the base toward the target while the adaptive anticipatory
scanner executes concurrently. EXPOSURE\_SCANNING executes the posture-adaptive
exposure body scan with integrated wound and burn detection. Upon completion,
EXPOSURE\_REVIEW enters an interactive phase where the operator can click any
grid point on the web camera view to command the robot to re-inspect that body
region. The review ends with a termination command, after which SCANNING
executes the \ac{FAST} cycle with pre-planned body reconfiguration between
targets.

\subsection{System Integration}

Fig.~\ref{fig:system_block} shows the block diagram of the complete system. The
Orbbec and RealSense \ac{RGBD} streams feed the perception pipeline
(\ac{YOLO}-pose, Kalman 3D, surface estimation). Detection and surface
estimates are routed to the mission \ac{FSM}, which coordinates three
controllers: a \ac{WBC} \ac{QP} for arm look-at during approach, an \ac{IK}
goal multiplexer for position-controlled trajectories, and the impedance
controller for compliant scanning. The \ac{WBC} \ac{QP} publishes base velocity
commands $[v_x,\, \omega_z]$ to Spot and arm goals to the multiplexer, while
the impedance controller publishes joint torques $\boldsymbol{\tau}$ directly
to the Z1. An \texttt{ik\_goal\_mux} node gives \ac{WBC} goals priority over
the Z1 \ac{FSM} goals when \ac{WBC} is enabled, ensuring seamless transitions
between the search, approach, and scanning phases.

The Z1 \ac{FSM} is integrated with the \ac{WBC} coordinator: when the approach
is complete and the anticipatory scanner has computed the \ac{FAST} target set,
the Z1 \ac{FSM} skips its internal scanning phase. During the \ac{FAST} cycle,
the coordinator optimises Spot's posture for each target and signals readiness;
the Z1 \ac{FSM} waits for this signal before executing the next target,
synchronising posture changes with arm motion. All components are available as
open-source software. An emergency stop immediately halts the base and returns
the arm to a safe configuration.

\subsection{Web-Based Operator Interface}

The \ac{TERESA} web interface provides dual-camera monitoring and robot control
via a browser dashboard communicating over rosbridge WebSocket. During exposure
review, the RealSense overlay renders the scan grid as clickable markers; each
click triggers a full re-positioning sequence---posture optimisation, settle,
and \ac{IK} trajectory replay---within the same \ac{ROS}~2 topic protocol used
for autonomous operation. A manual/automatic scan gate toggle allows the
operator to require explicit confirmation before advancing between mission
phases. All web commands are mirrored on the keyboard controller for
redundancy. Fig.~\ref{fig:web_interface} shows the interface during review.
