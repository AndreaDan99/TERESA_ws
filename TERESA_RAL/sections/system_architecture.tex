\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:system_architecture}

The \ac{TERESA} system integrates heterogeneous hardware and software
components over \ac{ROS}~2 with \ac{DDS}. This section describes how these
components are connected into a deployable field system, from the physical
platform through the coordination layer to the remote monitoring interface.
\subsection{Hardware Platform}
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/cad_callouts.pdf}
  \caption{Mechanical integration of the Z1 arm, Orbbec Femto Bolt, RealSense D415, and auxiliary battery onto Spot's payload frame.}
  \label{fig:cad_assembly}
\end{figure}
\draft{The \ac{TERESA} hardware platform builds on \emph{Spotzi}. To this base
configuration we added an Orbbec Femto Bolt body-mounted RGB-D camera for
wide-field patient detection, an Intel RealSense D415 wrist-mounted at
the Z1 end-effector for close-range torso inspection, an auxiliary
battery pack that powers the Z1 and the Orbbec independently of Spot's
internal supply, and an NVIDIA Jetson Orin AGX for onboard perception and
control. The entire assembly was designed in Fusion 360
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cad_assembly}) with a modular bracket system that allows components to be
easily swapped, removed, or extended without modifying the underlying
structure.
The centre of mass is placed slightly rearward of Spot's geometric centre
to counterbalance the arm's forward displacement when extended toward the
patient.} \draft{Several candidate arms were evaluated within the Fusion 360
assembly; the Z1 was selected for its light weight and compact form factor,
which preserve Spot's payload margin for the onboard computer and sensors
while avoiding interference with the base during locomotion.} Spot's five built-in stereo cameras are positioned low on the body
for ground-plane obstacle avoidance and are not suitable for skeleton detection.
\draft{Computation is distributed across two units: Spot's onboard computer
handles base control and odometry, while an NVIDIA Jetson Orin AGX runs
perception, arm control, task coordination, and \ac{WBC} via Docker
containers with CUDA 11.4.}

\subsection{Software Architecture}
\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/system_block_standalone.pdf}
  \caption{System block diagram. The architecture spans sensors, perception,
           \ac{FSM}, planning, control, and hardware. Dashed arrows denote
           operator-triggered or posture-optimisation paths.}
  \label{fig:system_block}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure*}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{figures/fsm_standalone.pdf}
  \caption{\ac{FSM} with 13 states. Dashed transitions are perception-driven;
           solid transitions are geometry-driven.}
  \label{fig:fsm}
\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:system_block} shows the complete system architecture. The Orbbec
and RealSense RGB-D streams feed the Perception layer, which runs YOLO-pose
skeleton detection, Kalman-filtered 3D back-projection, NLF 3D body pose
estimation, surface estimation, and Grounding DINO injury detection. Perception
outputs route to the 13-state \ac{FSM}, which enables four Planning modules: the
Exposure Scanner, the \ac{FAST} Ultrasound planner, the Body Pose Optimizer, and
the Web UI for interactive review. The Control layer translates plans into
actuation: the \ac{WBC} \ac{QP} publishes base velocity $[v_x,\,\omega_z]$ to
Spot and arm goals to the \ac{IK} Goal Multiplexer; the Impedance Controller
manages compliant contact during \ac{FAST} scanning, publishing joint torques
$\boldsymbol{\tau}$ directly to the Z1. The Z1 \ac{FSM} manages the arm's
internal state transitions---homing, look-at tracking, scanning trajectory
execution, and impedance-controlled contact---and synchronises with the
\ac{WBC} coordinator. Dashed arrows in the diagram denote operator-triggered or
posture-optimisation paths. An emergency stop immediately halts the base and
returns the arm to a safe configuration.

\subsection{Mission Management}
The four assessment phases of Section~\ref{sec:problem_formulation} are
orchestrated by a 13-state \ac{FSM} (Fig.~\ref{fig:fsm}). After Waiting TF and
Idle ensure system readiness, the \ac{FSM} advances through Searching (Phase~1),
Approaching (Phase~2), Exposure (Phase~3), and \acs{FAST} (Phase~4).
Perception confidence triggers dashed transitions; geometric conditions drive
solid edges. Timeouts at any point revert to the search, preventing commitment
to false detections. The \ac{FSM} serves as a pragmatic integration layer that
sequences the methods of Section~\ref{sec:active_perception} into a deployable
field system; alternative orchestration paradigms could extend this approach in
future work.

\subsection{Remote Monitoring}
\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/Web_interface}
  \caption{Web-based operator interface during the exposure scanning phase. The
           Body Map (top-down view) shows the patient mannequin skeleton with
           colour-coded body regions, the exposure scan grid points, and the Spot
           robot position (yellow). The info panel (top-left) displays Spot's
           current posture parameters. During exposure review, the operator can
           click any grid marker to command the robot to re-inspect that body
           region.}
  \label{fig:web_interface}
\end{figure}
Full autonomy in emergency assessment is neither feasible nor desirable: the
remote operator must retain the ability to override or re-inspect regions of
clinical interest. \draft{\ac{TERESA} bridges this gap with a web
interface that displays a top-down view of the patient mannequin and scan
grid---the Body Map. A single click on any grid marker in this map translates
into a 3D whole-body repositioning command (posture optimisation, base
navigation, and arm trajectory replay) without manual teleoperation.}

The \ac{TERESA} web interface provides dual-camera monitoring and robot control
via a browser-based dashboard. During exposure review, the RealSense overlay
renders the scan grid as clickable markers; each click triggers a full
re-positioning sequence---posture optimisation, settle, and \ac{IK} trajectory
replay---within the same \ac{ROS}~2 topic protocol used for autonomous
operation. \draft{A manual/automatic scan gate toggle allows the
operator to require explicit confirmation before advancing between phases.}
All web commands are mirrored on the keyboard controller for redundancy.
Fig.~\ref{fig:web_interface} shows the interface during review.
