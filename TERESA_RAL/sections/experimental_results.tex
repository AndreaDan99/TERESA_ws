\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:experimental_results}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

Experiments ran on the physical Spot+Z1 platform with an Orbbec Femto Bolt on
Spot's body and a RealSense D435 at the \ac{EE}, computing across SpotCore
and an onboard NVIDIA Jetson Orin AGX (\ac{ROS}~2 Humble, CUDA 11.4,
PyTorch 2.0).  Ten supine mannequin
positions were defined (\SI{2}{\metre}--\SI{4}{\metre}, $\pm\SI{45}{\degree}$
angular offset); each ran in both modes below, yielding twenty paired trials.

Neural network inference runs entirely on the Jetson GPU.
YOLO11n-pose skeleton detection operates at 8--14\,fps, NLF 3D body pose
estimation at 3--4\,fps (end-to-end), and Grounding DINO zero-shot wound
detection at $\sim$1\,fps.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Baseline}: sequential stop-and-manipulate. Fixed three-pose scan
        after navigation, no concurrency.
  \item \textbf{\ac{TERESA}}: anticipatory scanner overlapping navigation,
        adaptive grid, and QualityMonitor-modulated velocity.
\end{itemize}

Metrics: total mission time, idle time, scan overlap (fraction of body scan
completed before handoff), \ac{EE} positioning error at each \ac{FAST} point,
and lock time.

Across all twenty end-to-end trials, \ac{TERESA} reduced total mission time
from \SI{100.2 \pm 8.1}{\second} to \SI{89.7 \pm 7.3}{\second}
(\SI{10.5}{\percent}).  The gain came from eliminating idle time---the
anticipatory scanner completed $\sim$\SI{80}{\percent} of the body scan
concurrently with approach---and from the adaptive grid, which selected the
minimal two-pose pattern in the majority of trials.  Probe positioning error
dropped from \SI{44.7 \pm 5.2}{\milli\metre} to \SI{29.8 \pm 4.1}{\milli\metre}.
Note that the baseline benefits from an optimistic assumption: without body
reconfiguration, many \ac{FAST} targets are unreachable from the default
posture (Section~\ref{sec:body_reconf_results}).  Manual operation, where
operators skipped unreachable grid points, reduced total time to
$\sim$\SI{70}{\second} at the cost of incomplete coverage.  The principal
contribution of \ac{TERESA} is therefore not speed but capability: without
whole-body reconfiguration, the baseline simply cannot reach many anatomical
targets and returns incomplete scans with higher positioning error.
\ac{TERESA} achieves systematic, precise, and complete assessment across all
targets, which the baseline structurally cannot deliver regardless of the time
budget.

\subsection{Search Phase: Whole-Body Coverage and Dual-Sensor Lock}

We isolated the arm's contribution to search through an ablation experiment:
\ac{TERESA} (full system) versus a Spot-only baseline with the Z1 arm disabled
and only the front-facing Orbbec active. Twenty trials were conducted at five
relative mannequin orientations ($0^\circ$--$180^\circ$, two repetitions each), measuring
time to Orbbec posture confidence $\geq$ \num{0.70}.

With the arm's seven scanning poses providing full \SI{360}{\degree} coverage
as the base walked forward, \ac{TERESA} required no base rotation: the
mannequin was detected near-instantly at frontal orientations, with the rest found during the scan, yielding a
mean of \SI{3.2 \pm 1.1}{\second}. Without the arm, the fixed camera's
\SI{77}{\degree} effective field of view forced five discrete base rotations
($\sim$\SI{7}{\second} each at \SI{0.2}{\radian\per\second}, plus
\SI{2.0}{\second} dwell), giving \SI{14.8 \pm 2.3}{\second}---a
\textbf{5.0}$\times$ slowdown. A \num{10000}-trial Monte Carlo simulation
confirmed this ratio across all mannequin orientations.

The dual-sensor architecture added robustness independently of speed: in three
of the ten \ac{TERESA} trials, the Orbbec alone failed to lock. These failures
stemmed from the Orbbec's fixed horizontal viewpoint at
\SI{0.5}{\metre} above the ground, which is suboptimal for detecting a supine
body whose visual features---torso, limbs, head---are primarily visible from
above. The arm-mounted RealSense, positioned by the \ac{WBC} look-at controller
to observe the scene from an elevated angle, detected partial torso keypoints
in these cases and guided the base toward the body, giving the Orbbec a second
observation window at a more favourable orientation. Without this redundancy,
these trials would have resumed the search cycle, adding
$\sim$\SI{14}{\second}. The arm thus serves two complementary roles: its
\SI{360}{\degree} coverage eliminates the base-rotation bottleneck, while its
elevated viewpoint provides the sensor redundancy that prevents detection
failures a single-camera system would incur.

\subsection{Whole-Body Reconfiguration}
\label{sec:body_reconf_results}

The body posture optimizer described in Section~\ref{sec:body_reconf} proved
essential for reaching distant anatomical targets on a full-scale body.  We
compared four configurations of increasing capability, summarised in
Fig.~\ref{fig:posture_results}.  The fixed nominal posture represents Spot's
default standing pose (height \SI{0}{\metre}, pitch \SI{0}{\degree}, no base
displacement).  The heuristic posture is a single, empirically chosen
configuration---moderately lowered (\SI{-0.15}{\metre}) with a slight forward
pitch (\SI{5}{\degree})---that brings the arm closer to the ground and tilts
its workspace toward the patient.  While this improves coverage over the
nominal pose by exploiting the fact that all targets lie below the robot, it
cannot adapt to the varying depth and lateral position of individual points.
The 2D optimiser selects the best $(h, \varphi)$ per target via
kinematically-aware stance search with \ac{IK}-driven retry, adapting posture
to each point's location.  The 3D optimiser adds longitudinal base walking
$dy$ with the regularised cost
$J = \|\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{sweet}}\| + \lambda\,|dy|$
($\lambda = 0.50$).  The penalty encourages height and pitch adjustments over
walking---reducing energy consumption and producing smoother motion---while
still allowing displacement when necessary.  The residual \SI{15}{\percent} of
exposure points were at the furthest extremities (head, feet), beyond the
arm's maximum reach even with full base repositioning; these were skipped
gracefully.
