\section{Whole-Body Active Perception and Control}
\label{sec:active_perception}

\draft{The term \emph{whole-body} here denotes a perceptual extension
of the classical \ac{WBC} principle. Whereas traditional \ac{WBC} coordinates
all degrees of freedom to minimise kinematic error, \ac{TERESA} extends this
coordination to the perceptual domain: body posture and arm motion are treated
as explicit control resources that together shape the robot's sensory field.
By commanding posture to improve what the robot sees, from closer range and from more informative viewpoints, \ac{TERESA} elevates the legged base from a
passive transport platform to an active participant in the perception-action
loop. This principle underpins every phase of the mission, from searching
through approaching to scanning.}

\subsection{Searching}
\label{sec:phase_searching}

Before the robot can approach the patient, it must first localise them
reliably. In an unstructured emergency scenario, the patient's position is
unknown a priori. The \ac{EE}-mounted camera benefits from the Z1 arm's full
six-\ac{DOF} mobility, which can orient the camera in any direction without
moving the base. The body-mounted camera, however, has a fixed orientation
relative to Spot: its downward field of view depends entirely on body pitch,
making pitch the only posture parameter that directly improves its detection of
a patient lying on the ground. \ac{TERESA} therefore avoids time-consuming base
rotations and instead searches by walking forward while sweeping body pitch.

\subsubsection{Forward search with pitch sweep}

The robot walks forward at low speed while sweeping body pitch through a
sequence of angles $\varphi \in [0, \varphi_{\max}]$. The body-mounted camera
runs YOLO11-pose and a posture classifier, outputting a confidence score
$c \in [0, 1]$; the \ac{EE}-mounted camera runs a torso tracker that scans the
surroundings independently of the base heading. Algorithm~1 formalises the
confidence-gated search loop.
\draft{This strategy exploits the arm's native mobility for rotational
search while using body pitch---the one viewing parameter the arm cannot
compensate for---to optimise the body-mounted camera's fixed viewpoint. When a
lock is confirmed (Algorithm~\ref{alg:search}), the arm returns
home and the coordinator collects $N$ approach-point samples in
$\mathcal{F}_W$, computing their mean as the navigation target, while the NLF
pipeline accumulates skeleton estimates with exponential moving average
smoothing. If the detection is temporarily lost, the system tolerates a brief
absence before resuming the search.}

\begin{algorithm}[h!]
\caption{Confidence-Gated Search with Dual-Sensor Lock}
\label{alg:search}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]\small
\STATE \textbf{Input:} $\varphi_{\max}$, thresholds $\tau_{\text{high}}, \tau_{\text{low}}$, $N$ samples
\STATE \textbf{Output:} navigation target $\mathbf{p}_{\text{nav}} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, skeleton estimate
\WHILE{not locked}
  \FOR{$\varphi \in \{\varphi_1, \ldots, \varphi_{\max}\}$}
    \STATE $c_b \gets \textsc{BodyDetect}(\varphi)$
    \IF{$c_b > \tau_{\text{high}}$}
      \STATE $\textsc{ArmHome}()$
      \FOR{$i = 1$ \TO $N$}
        \STATE $\mathbf{p}_i \gets \textsc{GetApproachPoint}()$
        \STATE $\textsc{NLFAccumulate}(\mathbf{p}_i)$
      \ENDFOR
      \STATE $\mathbf{p}_{\text{nav}} \gets \textsc{Mean}(\{\mathbf{p}_i\})$
      \STATE \RETURN lock
    \ENDIF
    \STATE $c_e \gets \textsc{EEDetect}()$
    \IF{$c_e > \tau_{\text{low}}$}
      \STATE $\textsc{WalkToward}(\text{detection})$
      \STATE $\textsc{PitchRefine}(\varphi)$
    \ENDIF
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE $\textsc{AdvanceForward}()$
\ENDWHILE
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
\subsection{Approaching}
\label{sec:phase_approaching}

\draft{In traditional mobile manipulation pipelines, the robot first
navigates to a target, stops, and only then begins torso mapping---introducing
significant idle time. \ac{TERESA} eliminates this idle period by mapping the
torso concurrently with the approach. Algorithm~2 formalises the parallel
base-controller / torso-mapper execution.}

\begin{algorithm}[h!]
\caption{Anticipatory Approaching with Concurrent Torso Mapping}
\label{alg:approach}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]\small
\STATE \textbf{Input:} $\mathbf{p}_{\text{nav}} \in \mathcal{F}_W$, torso keypoint confidences
\STATE \textbf{Output:} fused torso model, \acs{FAST} target poses $\mathcal{T}$
\STATE \textbf{parallel do}
  \STATE \hspace{2mm}// Base navigation
  \WHILE{$\|\mathbf{p}_{\text{nav}} - \boldsymbol{\xi}\| > \varepsilon$}
    \STATE $v_x, \omega_z \gets \textsc{ProportionalCtrl}(\mathbf{p}_{\text{nav}}, \boldsymbol{\xi})$
    \STATE $v_x \gets \textsc{QualityMonitor}(v_x, \text{confidence})$
  \ENDWHILE
  \STATE \hspace{2mm}// Torso mapping
  \WHILE{not handoff}
    \STATE $\dot{\mathbf{q}} \gets \textsc{DampedPinv}(\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{arm}}, \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{des}})$ \hfill $\triangleright$ Eq.~\ref{eq:damped_pinv}
    \IF{all 4 keypoints confident}
      \STATE $\text{grid} \gets \text{2-pose minimal pattern}$
    \ELSE
      \STATE $\text{grid} \gets \text{3-pose comprehensive pattern}$
    \ENDIF
    \FOR{each pose $\in$ grid}
      \STATE $\textsc{FuseDepthSkeleton}(\text{pose})$
    \ENDFOR
  \ENDWHILE
\STATE \textbf{end parallel}
\STATE $\mathcal{T} \gets \textsc{FASTTargetOffsets}(\text{fused\_torso})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\draft{The arm controller orients the \ac{EE}-mounted camera toward the
patient's torso centroid. Joint velocities are computed via the damped
pseudo-inverse of the arm Jacobian:}
\begin{equation}
\dot{\mathbf{q}} = \mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{arm}}^\top
\bigl(\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{arm}} \mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{arm}}^\top
+ \mu\,\mathbf{I}_6\bigr)^{-1} \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{des}},
\label{eq:damped_pinv}
\end{equation}
\draft{where $\mu > 0$ prevents singularities near kinematic limits.
Only the angular component of $\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{des}}$ is active: it encodes
the look-at rotation $\mathbf{x}_{EE} = (\mathbf{p}^* -
\mathbf{p}_{EE})/\|\mathbf{p}^* - \mathbf{p}_{EE}\|$, keeping the torso centred
in the frame; the translational component is zero, holding a nominal home
position. The base approach speed $v_x$ is modulated by the detection
confidence: the robot slows when the torso estimate is uncertain and resumes
full speed once confidence is restored. We refer to this confidence-gated
modulation as the QualityMonitor. At each viewpoint, depth
and skeleton data are fused into 3D keypoint estimates via a weighted average
of detection rate and spatial stability. From the fused torso model, the five
canonical \ac{FAST} target poses are computed as fixed offsets from the torso
centroid in $\mathcal{F}_0$.}

\subsection{Exposure}
\label{sec:phase_exposure}

After the torso mapper has localised the patient's anatomy, Phase~3
executes a systematic visual inspection of the body surface, serving the ``E''
(Exposure) component of the \ac{ABCDE} protocol. A key design principle is that
the \draft{remote} operator remains in the loop: the system automates the scanning motion but
preserves the clinician's ability to inspect, re-examine, and override.

\subsubsection{Posture optimisation for body reconfiguration}
\label{sec:body_reconf}

Once the anticipatory torso mapper has localised the patient's anatomy and
computed the target set $\mathcal{T}$ (both \ac{FAST} ultrasound targets and
exposure grid points) in $\mathcal{F}_W$, the system pre-plans an optimal body
posture for each target through a two-tier kinematically-aware stance search
with \ac{IK}-driven retry. This optimisation is shared with Phase~4
(Section~\ref{sec:phase_fast}).

The posture optimisation space spans three parameters: body height $h \in
[h_{\min}, h_{\max}]$ and pitch $\varphi \in [0, \varphi_{\max}]$, as defined
in Section~\ref{sec:problem_formulation}, plus a body-frame base displacement
$dy \in \mathbb{R}$ along the patient's head-to-foot axis, expressed in the
patient-aligned body frame (Section~\ref{sec:system_architecture}).
The discrete search grids are $\mathcal{H} = \{h_1, \dots, h_{N_h}\}$ and
$\mathcal{P} = \{\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_{N_p}\}$, with $dy$ sampled
uniformly at \SI{0.10}{\metre} intervals.  The cost function includes a
regularisation term that penalises lateral displacement:
\begin{equation}
  J(h, \varphi, dy) =
  \bigl\|\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{F}_W}^{\mathcal{F}_0}(h, \varphi, dy)\,
  \mathbf{p}^* - \mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{sweet}}\bigr\|
  + \lambda\,|dy|,
  \label{eq:posture_cost}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{sweet}} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the empirically
determined centre of the Z1 arm's preferred manipulation region in
$\mathcal{F}_0$, and $\lambda = 0.50$ weights lateral movement against
reachability.  The value $\lambda=0.50$ was chosen empirically to balance
reachability against energy cost: $\lambda>1.0$ prevented necessary base
displacement, while $\lambda<0.25$ caused excessive walking for marginal
reachability gains.  This penalty encourages the optimiser to prefer height and pitch
adjustments over base walking---reducing energy consumption and producing
smoother, more predictable motion---while still allowing longitudinal
displacement when strictly necessary.  The two-tier escalation proceeds as
follows:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{\draft{In-place search}}---$(h, \varphi)$ only: a
        coarse grid sweeps $\mathcal{H} \times \mathcal{P}$ ($N_h N_p$
        combinations). The posture yielding an \ac{IK} solution within
        \SI{2}{\second} is selected.
  \item \textbf{\draft{Base-displacement search}}---$(dy, h,
        \varphi)$: when the in-place search fails, the optimiser adds
        longitudinal base displacement $dy$, expanding to $N_{dy} N_h N_p$
        combinations. If the \ac{IK} solver times out, the point is skipped.
\end{enumerate}

The optimisation is computed once per target after $\mathcal{T}$ is available,
imposing no real-time computational burden.  Body reconfiguration is executed
between targets: Spot assumes the pre-computed posture $(h_i^*, \varphi_i^*)$,
settles, and the base navigator drives the $dy$ displacement before the Z1 arm
executes the \ac{IK} trajectory toward the transformed target.

\subsubsection{Posture-adaptive scan grid}

The scan grid adapts to the patient's estimated body pose. Four torso
keypoints---left and right shoulders, left and right hips---define a bilinear
patch over the body surface.  Let $\mathbf{p}_{SL}, \mathbf{p}_{SR},
\mathbf{p}_{HL}, \mathbf{p}_{HR} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ denote these keypoints in
$\mathcal{F}_W$.  The grid is generated by interpolating uniformly along the
two parametric directions: head-to-foot ($u$-axis, from shoulders to hips) and
lateral ($v$-axis, from left to right).  A grid point at parameters
$(u, v) \in [0,1] \times [0,1]$ is computed as
$\mathbf{p}(u,v) = (1{-}u)(1{-}v)\,\mathbf{p}_{SL} + u(1{-}v)\,\mathbf{p}_{HL}
+ (1{-}u)v\,\mathbf{p}_{SR} + uv\,\mathbf{p}_{HR}$, with a fixed height offset
to maintain a safe camera-to-skin distance.  Typical grid resolution is
\SI{0.15}{\metre} in both directions, producing 15--25 points depending on
torso dimensions.  When the NLF prior is available, all 24 \ac{SMPL} joints
are populated, extending the grid to the head, hands, and feet for full-body
coverage.  Grid points are visited in a serpentine path to minimise arm
travel.

\subsubsection{Injury detection}

At each grid point, Grounding DINO~\cite{liu2023groundingdino}---a zero-shot
open-vocabulary object detector---runs on the \draft{EE-mounted camera} RGB stream at
approximately 1\,fps on the Jetson Orin GPU, prompted with a vocabulary
of wound-related terms (wound, laceration, bleeding, burn, etc.).
\draft{We select Grounding DINO over alternatives such as
OWL-ViT~\cite{minderer2022owlv2} and GLIP~\cite{li2022glip} because its joint
visual-language grounding produces tighter bounding boxes for fine-grained
injury descriptions, which is critical for 3D back-projection accuracy.} \draft{Detections are projected to 3D world coordinates via the
registered depth channel and associated with their grid point index. This 3D
projection is required because the operator's clicks during interactive review
trigger a physical robot repositioning to the corresponding body surface
point, which demands metric 3D coordinates.}

\subsubsection{Web-based interactive review}

\draft{Following the sweep, the web interface
(Section~\ref{sec:system_architecture}) overlays the scan grid on the
\ac{EE}-mounted camera feed with detected injuries highlighted as
colour-coded bounding boxes. The remote operator can click any grid point
to command a full re-inspection of that body region.}

\subsection{\acs{FAST}}
\label{sec:phase_fast}

\draft{Phase~4 performs autonomous ultrasound acquisition at the $N$
\ac{FAST} targets estimated during Phase~2. Body posture optimisation for each
$\mathbf{T}_i^* \in \mathcal{T}$ follows the same two-tier procedure described
in Section~\ref{sec:body_reconf}. The critical challenge of this phase is
compliant probe contact: the ultrasound probe must maintain controlled pressure
against the patient's skin---excessive force risks injury, while insufficient
contact loses the acoustic signal. On a legged platform, this is compounded by
the fact that the Z1 arm provides no native impedance control, requiring us to
implement a custom torque-level controller for safe physical interaction.}

\subsubsection{Contact control}

\draft{Ultrasound scanning requires compliant probe contact. To
achieve this on our platform, \ac{TERESA} implements a custom Cartesian
impedance controller on the Z1 torque interface at \SI{500}{\hertz}.}
The desired \ac{EE} pose is computed relative to the torso surface centroid
$\mathbf{p}_s$ and outward normal $\mathbf{n}_s$, placing the probe at
$\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{des}} = \mathbf{p}_s + d\,\mathbf{n}_s$, where $d$ is the
desired contact offset.  Joint torques are computed as
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{arm}}(\mathbf{q})^\top
\bigl[\mathbf{K}_p(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{des}} - \mathbf{p}_{EE})
- \mathbf{K}_d\,\dot{\mathbf{p}}_{EE}\bigr]
+ \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{q}) + \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}}),
\label{eq:impedance}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{K}_p, \mathbf{K}_d \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ are adaptive
stiffness and damping gains that scale linearly with arm extension to prevent
overloading at the workspace boundary, $\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{q})$ and
$\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}})$ are the gravity and Coriolis terms
computed via Pinocchio~\cite{carpentier2019pinocchio}, and
$\dot{\mathbf{p}}_{EE} = \mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{arm}}(\mathbf{q})\,
\dot{\mathbf{q}}$ is the end-effector velocity.  A safe switching service
alternates between the impedance controller and a position-controlled joint
trajectory controller, defaulting to position control on error.
